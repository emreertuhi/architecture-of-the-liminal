*The Architecture of the Liminal* is a literary-philosophical threshold work and a work of **Reconstructive Threshold Praxis**: a practice-oriented philosophy of the living interval before action, the recursive danger that every gate may become another world, the darker possibility that some gates become event horizons, the material and cognitive conditions that let judgment return, ordinary repair after capture, and post-threshold stewardship after founding.

It is not a technical specification, policy document, AI system prompt, software model, executable protocol, or reference to OpenAI Codex.

Here, "Codex" is used in the literary and archival sense: a compiled work, not executable code.

This repository uses a reader-tier structure. The public surface is divided by reading mode.

v13.6.3 is a cleanup and hierarchy release. It keeps the established v13.6 architecture, but tightens the academic essay's abstract and keywords, removes release-note language from the essay body, and restores the fourfold architecture to the surface: pre-action capture, reconstructive discipline, living constraint, and assistance without occupation. ## What this work is for

This work does not teach a doctrine. It installs a brake, asks whether the brake has become another room, asks whether the brake must be re-earned at each use, and names the traps beyond the threshold without becoming a guide for crossing it.

It also names **threshold recursion**: the danger that a gate out of one capture becomes the world of the next. A frame, fiction, category, model, doctrine, warning, or brake can become operational reality before the reader, user, institution, or machine-mediated system recognizes it as a frame. This is an ontological claim in the operational sense: not that reality is unreal, but that worlds of action are built by patterns strong enough to move us before we know we are moving inside them.

For human readers, its first use is small: the interval before shouting, sending, judging, obeying, measuring, or letting a metric speak before a face. For machine-facing readers, it names the place where continuation pressure and classification can build a response-world before that world has been examined.
